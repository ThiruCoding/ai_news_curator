Ludi Akue highlights a growing challenge: the rapid expansion of AI, particularly generative AI, is creating significant environmental strain due to high energy consumption, accelerated hardware churn (GPU lifespan of 2-3 years), and a lack of cost transparency for users. While technical solutions like model compression and novel architectures offer some mitigation, Akue's work at Bpifrance demonstrates that these improvements are often offset by increased AI usage, a phenomenon known as the rebound effect. She emphasizes the need for a fundamental shift in perspective, prioritizing questions of "should we?" over "can we?" and integrating environmental considerations into AI governance and product development to avoid exacerbating the climate crisis.

Liquid AI is challenging the conventional "scaling laws" in generative AI, which prioritize larger parameter counts for increased intelligence, with the release of LFM2.5-350M. This 350-million parameter model achieves remarkable "intelligence density" through extensive pre-training (28 trillion tokens) and reinforcement learning, outperforming larger models on specific benchmarks. Its technical innovation lies in a hybrid architecture utilizing Linear Input-Varying Systems (LIVs) and Grouped Query Attention (GQA) blocks, significantly reducing memory overhead and enabling efficient local deployment on edge devices like smartphones and Raspberry Pi's while maintaining a 32k context window and high throughput. The model is optimized for agentic tasks like instruction following and tool use, demonstrating a shift towards efficient, specialized AI rather than solely pursuing ever-larger models.
