Emerging AI models, particularly Anthropic's forthcoming "Mythos" model, pose a significant and escalating cybersecurity threat, prompting warnings from AI and government officials. These advanced models enable autonomous agents with unprecedented hacking capabilities, potentially leading to large-scale cyberattacks as early as this year, and are projected to outpace defensive efforts. The risk is compounded by the increasing use of AI agents like Claude and Copilot by employees, often unknowingly connecting to internal systems and creating vulnerabilities exploited by malicious actors, a phenomenon known as "shadow AI." This shift allows even single individuals to launch sophisticated campaigns previously requiring large teams, fundamentally changing the scale and accessibility of cyberattacks.

Microsoft AI has launched three new foundational AI models—MAI-Transcribe-1 (text transcription), MAI-Voice-1 (audio generation), and MAI-Image-2 (video generation)—expanding its multimodal AI capabilities and directly competing with rivals like Google and OpenAI. Developed by the newly formed MAI Superintelligence team led by Mustafa Suleyman, these models are positioned as a more cost-effective alternative, with pricing starting at $0.36 per hour for transcription. Despite this move, Microsoft maintains its significant partnership and $13 billion investment in OpenAI, leveraging both internal development and external partnerships, mirroring its strategy in the chip market. The release underscores Microsoft’s broader ambition to build "Humanist AI" integrated into its products and experiences.

Google is addressing developer feedback and advancing its open-weight AI offerings with the release of Gemma 4, succeeding the aging Gemma 3.
