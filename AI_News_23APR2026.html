The startup's platform utilizes AI agents and deterministic algorithms to interpret complex mass spectrometry data, a crucial but time-consuming process for assessing molecules. Founded by scientists with expertise in biology, chemistry, and AI, 10x Science aims to provide a SaaS solution for pharmaceutical companies and researchers, streamlining drug development and potentially unlocking new insights into molecular intelligence, independent of specific drug outcomes.

Google unveiled its "Agentic Enterprise" strategy at Cloud Next '26, centered around scaling AI capabilities for businesses. The core technical driver is a shift in TPU design, splitting the eighth-generation into training (TPU 8t) and inference (TPU 8i) chips to address rising inference demands and prioritizing massive scale—linking up to one million chips—over individual chip performance, surpassing Nvidia's interconnect capabilities. Complementing this is the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, simplifying autonomous agent creation with long-term memory and security features, alongside Workspace Intelligence, a unified layer connecting data across Google's productivity apps to enable more contextually aware AI interactions.

Google Cloud unveiled its eighth generation of Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), splitting them into TPU 8t for training and TPU 8i for inference, promising up to 3x faster training and 80% better performance per dollar. While these custom chips offer increased compute efficiency and cost savings, Google isn't replacing Nvidia entirely, instead supplementing its Nvidia-based systems and even collaborating with Nvidia to optimize networking technology like Falcon. This strategic approach reflects a broader trend among hyperscalers like Amazon and Microsoft, who are developing their own AI chips to meet growing demand while still leveraging Nvidia's established market dominance. Ultimately, Google's AI cloud growth is likely to benefit Nvidia, not hinder it.
