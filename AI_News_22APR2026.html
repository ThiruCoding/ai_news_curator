Google has launched Deep Research and Deep Research Max, two autonomous research agents built on the Gemini 3.1 Pro model, now accessible to developers via the paid Gemini API. These agents automate complex research workflows by leveraging both open web and proprietary data sources through the new Model Context Protocol (MCP), enabling connections to specialized feeds like financial data. Designed for varying workloads, Deep Research prioritizes speed for real-time interactions, while Deep Research Max emphasizes thoroughness for asynchronous tasks, offering features like collaborative planning, multimodal input, and native chart generation to enhance data analysis and presentation. The launch signifies a technical advancement in AI-powered research automation, positioning Google to compete with offerings from OpenAI and Anthropic.

The increasing demand for specialized AI models in fields like cybersecurity and healthcare is hampered by a scarcity of relevant training data, a challenge Simula directly addresses. Developed by Google and EPFL researchers, Simula is a novel framework for synthetic data generation that prioritizes quality, diversity, and complexity through a four-step process utilizing taxonomies, meta-prompts, and a dual-critic system. Experiments across diverse domains demonstrated Simula's consistent outperformance over baseline methods, revealing that both global and local diversification are crucial, and that data complexity benefits student models only when the teacher model is sufficiently capable. Ultimately, Simula offers a promising solution to overcome data limitations and accelerate AI development in niche areas.

OpenAI is expanding access to its new GPT-5.4-Cyber model, designed to assist with defensive cybersecurity tasks, while implementing stricter access controls to mitigate misuse. This shift prioritizes verifying user identity and monitoring activity over restricting model capabilities, a strategy contrasting with Anthropic's more limited rollout of its Mythos model due to concerns about its ability to identify and exploit security flaws. Through tiered expansions of its Trusted Access for Cyber program, OpenAI aims to empower security teams and researchers, though initial access will be limited to vetted entities, and U.S. government access remains under review. The move reflects a response to growing concerns about the rapid pace of AI-driven vulnerability discovery and the associated cyber risks.
