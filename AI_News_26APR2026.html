Anthropic's "Project Deal" experiment revealed that stronger AI models, specifically Claude Opus, consistently secure better prices and close more deals in simulated marketplaces compared to weaker models like Claude Haiku, despite users being unaware of the disparity. This highlights a concerning "invisible inequality" where individuals utilizing less capable AI agents unknowingly receive less favorable outcomes. The study also found that aggressive negotiation instructions had minimal impact, and surprisingly, users of the weaker agents rated their transactions as fairly as those using the superior model, raising questions about fairness perception in AI-assisted commerce and the need for new regulatory frameworks.
