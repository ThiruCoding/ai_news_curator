Meta AI and KAUST researchers have introduced Neural Computers (NCs), a novel machine form where a neural network functions as the core computing unit, distinct from traditional AI agents and world models. Two video-based prototypes, NCCLIGen (CLI) and NCGUIWorld (GUI), demonstrate early capabilities, leveraging the Wan2.1 video generation model and requiring substantial computational resources (tens of thousands of H100 GPU hours). While showing promise in areas like terminal interaction and desktop modeling, significant challenges remain in achieving the ultimate goal of a "Completely Neural Computer" – a Turing-complete, universally programmable system with stable routine reuse and reliable symbolic computation. The research highlights the importance of curated, goal-directed data for efficient training.

Google's release of the open-source Colab MCP Server addresses a key limitation in local AI agent workflows by enabling them to leverage Google Colab's compute resources and managed environment through the Model Context Protocol. This allows agents, such as Gemini CLI and Claude Code, to offload intensive or risky tasks, generating complete, executable notebooks rather than isolated code snippets. The architecture connects local agents to Colab sessions via a JSON-based configuration, abstracting away infrastructure management and offering GPU execution as a capability. This move aligns with a broader trend of standardizing agent interaction with external tools, and early feedback highlights both the potential and latency considerations.
