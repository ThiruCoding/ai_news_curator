A recent Stanford study challenges the prevailing assumption that multi-agent AI systems inherently outperform single agents, revealing that their apparent advantage often stems from utilizing significantly more computational resources. Researchers found that when given equal compute, single agents typically perform as well or better than teams due to information loss during inter-agent handoffs. However, teams demonstrate an advantage when dealing with corrupted or lengthy contexts, effectively filtering noise and benefiting from diverse perspectives, particularly when built on less powerful base models. The findings suggest that while multi-agent systems offer potential, optimizing single agent context handling remains crucial for efficient and effective AI reasoning.
